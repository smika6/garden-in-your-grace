A Garden in Your Grace

for Tay

Start at dawn, just above,
and read clockwise.

The hand keeps real time.
Tap or hover a part to move it.

Dawn

morning glory

right now

Morning glories open before anyone asks them to.

You are not a morning glory.

You are a tangle of blankets,

a hand swatting the alarm,

a grumble from somewhere under all that hair.

But wait long enough

and the smile comes anyway,

slow, sleepy, sweet,

climbing whatever part of me

is closest to the light.

And here’s what the morning glory never learned:

it closes by noon.

Yours stays open all day.

Late Morning

sunflower

right now

By the time you make it outside,

the dew is long gone, and you don’t care.

Knees in the dirt, hair tied back and coming loose,

gold and brown, the exact colors

of a sunflower’s face.

And you turn the way it turns,

toward whatever is alive and needs you:

the basil, the seedlings, the friend who called last night,

me.

Noon

zinnia

right now

August burns the fields to rust,

and the zinnias don’t care.

They bloom on, faithful, as the true ones must.

That’s how you stay.

And your laugh is like that too,

color thrown hard against the gray,

loud and unbothered.

Golden Hour

marigold

right now

Gardeners plant marigolds along the border

to keep what’s tender safe.

You do that.

You stand around the ones you love like prayer,

a low gold fire,

holding the worries of the world at bay.

Dusk

rose

right now

Your hand finds mine on the walk back in,

and there it is, the rose of you:

soft, yes,

but with a steady will beneath it,

a little thorn

for anyone who’d hurt what’s yours,

and somehow that makes it sweeter.

Night

dahlia

right now

A dahlia is a hundred petals folded, row on row,

and that is the depth of you.

Even now, talking in the dark,

I find another layer new

and fall for that one too.

So if they ask me where the flowers grow,

I’ll tell them there’s no need to search some place.

No meadow ever put on such a show

as this whole garden growing in your grace.
